How Age Is Calculated: The Math Behind It

Most people assume age calculation is simple arithmetic. Subtract one year from another and you're done. But accurate age calculation — the kind used in legal documents, medical contexts, and precision tools like AgeVault — is far more nuanced.

Step 1: Compute the Raw Year Difference

Begin with: years = currentYear − birthYear. This gives an approximate age, but it doesn't account for whether the birthday has occurred this year yet.

Step 2: Adjust for the Month

If the current month is less than the birth month, the birthday hasn't happened yet this year, so subtract 1 from years and add 12 to the month difference. If the months are equal, proceed to day comparison.

Step 3: Adjust for the Day

If the current day is less than the birth day and months are equal, the birthday hasn't occurred yet in the current month. Subtract 1 from months (borrowing from years if needed) and add the actual number of days in the previous month to the day difference.

Example: Born March 28, 1990. Today is March 15, 2026.
Years = 2026 − 1990 = 36. But month is equal (March) and day 15 < 28 → subtract 1 → 35 years, plus 11 months and (28−15 = 13 days short, so: 30−13 = 17 days in Feb remaining) … → carefully borrow and count.

Total Days Calculation

For total days, AgeVault uses Unix timestamps — the number of milliseconds since January 1, 1970. The difference in milliseconds divided by (1000 × 60 × 60 × 24) gives total days. This is the most accurate method and automatically handles all leap years.

Total Hours, Minutes, Seconds

These are simply totalDays × 24, × 60, and × 60 respectively. Since we don't know your exact birth time, we assume midnight of your birth date, which gives the standard calendar-day-based result.

The Month-End Edge Case

What is the month difference between January 31 and March 1? It's 1 month and 1 day (in a non-leap year) or 1 month and 0 days (in a leap year). Calendar arithmetic must use the actual days in each month — not a fixed 30-day approximation.

Gregorian vs Other Calendars

AgeVault uses the Gregorian calendar, which is the international standard. For very old historical dates (before 1582), the Julian calendar was in use, and some dates differ by up to 13 days. AgeVault does not attempt to handle pre-Gregorian dates.

Why It Matters

Accurate age affects pension eligibility, passport applications, school enrollment cutoffs, medical dosing in pediatrics, and insurance premiums. Using an imprecise method can produce results that are off by a full day — which in edge cases matters legally.
